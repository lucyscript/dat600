\subsection*{c. Remove one edge to get a DAG}
Removing \((F, B)\) breaks every directed cycle in Figure~1.
One alphabetical topological order is \(A \rightarrow B \rightarrow C \rightarrow D \rightarrow E \rightarrow F \rightarrow G \rightarrow H \rightarrow J \rightarrow I\).
\subsection*{d. Transform a directed graph into a DAG}
Run DFS and remove every back edge.
On Figure~1 plus \(I \rightarrow C\) and \(C \rightarrow A\), the removed back edges are \((C, A)\), \((F, B)\), \((I, C)\).
The resulting graph is a DAG.
Running time: \(O(V + E)\).
\section{Problem Cable Network}
\subsection*{a. Budget \(b = 30\)}
The MST has cost \( 26 \) with edges \((A, D, 1)\), \((C, D, 2)\), \((D, E, 2)\), \((B, D, 4)\), \((D, F, 4)\), \((C, G, 6)\), \((F, H, 7)\).
Hence it is possible to connect all neighborhoods within the budget.
\subsection*{b. Degree limit on D}
With the additional constraint \(deg(D) \le 3\), the cheapest feasible spanning tree has cost \( 31 \) with edges \((A, D, 1)\), \((C, D, 2)\), \((D, E, 2)\), \((A, B, 5)\), \((C, G, 6)\), \((F, H, 7)\), \((B, H, 8)\).
The budget \(30\) is not achievable.
The degree-aware Kruskal heuristic produces cost \( 31 \).
A counterexample shows the heuristic is not always globally optimal: heuristic cost \( 13 \), optimal cost \( 12 \).
\subsection*{c. One weight swap under budget \(b' = 25\)}
Swapping \((A, B, 5)\) with \((C, G, 6)\) yields an MST of cost \( 25 \).
The best single swap found by exhaustive search gives cost \( 24 \).

\section{Problem Maximum Flow}
\subsection*{a. Resolving the antiparallel edge}
The antiparallel pair is \(V1 \rightarrow V3\) and \(V3 \rightarrow V1\).
Replace \(V3 \rightarrow V1\) (6) with \(V3 \rightarrow V3\_to\_V1\) (6) and \(V3\_to\_V1 \rightarrow V1\) (6).

\subsection*{c. Bottleneck via cuts}
A minimum cut is \(S = \{V1, V2, V3, V4, V5, s\}\) and \(T = \{t\}\).
